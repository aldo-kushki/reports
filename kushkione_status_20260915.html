Summary

Where we stand. 27 clients in production, of which 26 are Standalone and a single one runs on an integrated mode: siPOS, App to App Sync, in Chile. Volume. 1,968 transactions and USD 28,038.81 of TPV in the last seven days, with a 90.75% approval rate. siPOS accounts for 1,685 of those transactions, 86% of the total, so everything else is still pilot-sized: Colombia adds 111, Mexico 78, Peru 70 and Standalone in Chile 24. Clients in integration report no transactions, which is expected: they are still implementing the solution.

Blockers. Three critical issues are open, all of them already fixed and scheduled for the release of 15/09/2026, none confirmed in production yet. One sits on DMS, which every operation mode depends on, and the other two reach every service, so today every cell in both matrices is red. Once that release is confirmed in production, and with the volume we have today, App to App Sync in Chile and Standalone in Colombia become ready to scale, together with Charge and Other Services in Chile and Charge and Refunds in Colombia. Ten more high issues are reported for information: they do not affect clients in production and do not affect approval to scale; three ship on 15/09/2026, three on 25/09/2026 and four have no release date yet.

Releases. 37 tickets across the last four releases, 30 approved on first review (81%), 6 calendar days on average to production. Quality and speed are improving: 74% approved and 17 days to production on 21/08, versus 90% and 4 days on 03/09; hotfixes shipped in 1–2 days.

Pipeline. 16 projected clients covering 21 country slots: Mexico 8, Chile 6, Peru 5, Colombia 2, concentrated in App to App Sync and Cloud Sync. Seven names are new since the previous cut-off — Guaraná Motors, Klyns Farmacias, Foodbot (Mexico and Peru), Invupos, Zetus, Xetus and Talant — and the demand is clearly moving towards integrated modes, which are precisely the ones with no production client yet outside Chile.

Reporting criteria

Purpose of this block. It records every rule agreed for this report so it can be rebuilt from scratch from the spreadsheet without any other context. Sections, in order: Summary (a short narrative only — the KPI cards were removed on 2026-09-11, so no metric blocks belong here) → Country status → Product readiness by operation mode → Product readiness by service → Release status → Issues detected → this block.

Source. Google spreadsheet 1qHi_HphqZV8yhYy0Gza1FZMRX9xx9N9qTex1pCbSb0o, tabs Clientes (gid 0), Pipeline, Issues (gid 1543440227) and releases (gid 130905531). Read them as CSV through /gviz/tq?tqx=out:csv&gid=… (the /export?format=csv endpoint fails). Sheet dates are MM/DD/YYYY; this report always shows DD/MM/YYYY. The report is a single self-contained HTML file, in English, and always says “clients”, never “merchants”; all figures are rendered from the REPORT_DATA object, so refreshing data means editing that object only. This edition was generated on 2026-09-15 from a full re-read of the four tabs.

Countries and columns. Countries are Mexico, Chile, Colombia and Peru (the sheet writes México and Perú). Operation modes are Standalone, Cloud Sync, Cloud Async, App to App Sync and App to App Async. The readiness matrices show only the first four: App to App Async is excluded because both apps run on the same terminal, so asynchronous calls add little value there and gaps in that mode are expected rather than a problem to solve. Services keep the four report names Charge, Preauth & Capture, Refunds and Other Services, mapped from the sheet as Charge → Charge, Void → Refunds, Other Integration Services → Other Services.

Client counting. Statuses are Producción → Production, Integración → Integration, Abandona → Churned, plus Pipeline from its own tab. Every client row in Clientes now carries exactly one country and one operation mode. Within a country each client name counts once; regional totals count unique client names, so a client present in several countries counts once regionally and the sum across countries is higher. Churned clients are excluded from every count and only mentioned in the narrative. Pipeline clients never count towards readiness, because they have not started integrating; pipeline rows with several countries are expanded into one row per country, so 16 pipeline clients occupy 21 country slots.

Readiness criteria (per country × operation mode and per country × service). C1: at least one client in production with that mode or service. C2: those clients ran at least 100 transactions in the last 7 days (the threshold was lowered from 200 to 100 on 2026-09-15, once real volume was loaded). C3: no critical issue open for that country and that mode or service. States: green ✓ when all three are met, amber ! when at least one is missing, red ✕ when no criterion is met or when critical issues exist (issues force red on their own), and violet ⊘ “not started” when there are no clients at all in integration or production. That last state is a gap, not a neutral value: nothing has been piloted in that combination yet, which is why it does not use a plain grey dash. Red and amber cells print every reason under the icon, one short line each, and for issues that line is only the count plus the date they are all resolved (or “resolution date TBD”). The ticket-level detail belongs to the panel that opens when the cell is clicked: clients in production and in integration, clients in pipeline, weekly transactions against the threshold, a ✓/✕ checklist of the three criteria, and the list of open issues with ID, name, criticality, status, release date and the note “(DMS, applies to all operation modes)” where it applies. Each matrix has 16 cells: 4 countries × 4 operation modes and 4 countries × 4 services. There is no aggregate coverage percentage in the report.

Transactions. Volume comes from the columns “Transacciones en los últimos 7 días”, “TPV últimos 7 días” and “% de aceptación” of the Clientes tab, per client. A cell adds up the transactions of its production clients only. Clients in integration are expected to have no volume — they are still implementing — so an empty cell there is read as “no volume yet”, never as missing data; a production client with an empty volume column is counted as zero and flagged. A production client with 0 transactions still satisfies C1 but contributes nothing to C2, so an inactive pilot cannot make a mode ready. Transactions cannot be split across the services a client uses, so in the service matrix a client's whole volume counts towards each of its services; read those figures as “volume of the clients that offer this service”, not as volume of the service. Approval rate is averaged weighting by transactions, and only over clients that have both a rate and transactions; where no client in a segment has a rate the figure shows ⏳ awaiting data.

Issues. Critical means Immediate or Urgent (constant CRITICAL): these are the only issues that fail criterion 3. High issues are reported in the issues section for information and never affect approval to scale; together with the critical ones they form the reported set (constant REPORTED). Medium tickets stay in the tracker and out of this report. Status is irrelevant to whether an issue blocks: Fixed still has to pass QA and ship, so only the release date matters. An empty release date means TBD (to be defined) and ranks after every real date, so a cell that holds a TBD ticket cannot show a resolution date and reads “Resolution date TBD”. An issue applies to a cell when its country list contains the country and its mode or service list contains the column. DMS rule: DMS is stored literally in the sheet but is not a column here, because every operation mode requires it, so an issue whose mode is DMS is expanded to all operation modes (constant DMS_BLOCKS_CELLS, set to true; setting it to false would instead treat DMS issues as a cross-cutting note). Issues with no mode or service cannot be placed in any cell and appear in the issues table only.

Outlook line under each matrix. Every critical ticket in this edition is scheduled for 15/09/2026, the cut-off date itself, and none is confirmed in production, so all of them still block. To keep the matrices useful each one carries an outlook line that re-evaluates the same 16 cells ignoring critical tickets whose release date is on or before the cut-off date, i.e. the picture the moment that release is confirmed in production. It is an outlook, not a state: nothing turns green in the matrix until the release is confirmed.

Country status. One table, two rows per country plus a regional pair: Standalone covers clients using the Standalone mode and Integrated covers clients using Cloud Sync, Cloud Async, App to App Sync or App to App Async. Clients in production, in integration and pipeline split by segment; transactions, TPV and approval rate add up the clients of that segment. A segment with clients but no volume at all reads “No volume yet” (typically a segment where every client is still integrating) and a segment with no clients in production or integration reads “–”, because there is nothing to measure. “Operation modes live” and “Services live” require at least one client in production, not just integrating, and the regional row shows the union of everything live in any country.

Release status. Days to production = production shipping date minus first delivery date, in calendar days. First-pass approval = tickets approved on the first review ÷ total tickets. Average time to production averages those days over the releases listed. Shipping to production does not imply a per-terminal deployment status.

Open data gaps and assumptions. El Taller Orgánico (Colombia, in production) has a MID but no transactions, TPV or approval rate, and is counted as zero. No client in Mexico has an approval rate, so Mexico shows ⏳ awaiting data in that column. Justo now appears as three rows — Justo Chile, Justo México and Justo Colombia — with their own mode and services filled in, so the earlier assumption that Mexico and Colombia mirrored Chile is no longer needed. K1-304 lists all four countries while its title mentions Mexico only; the Payrillium report of 09/09/2026 also states all four, so it is reported as all four. The five tickets with no date carry an empty cell in the sheet instead of the literal “TBD”, and are read as TBD here. Two tickets, K1-286 and K1-311, are recorded as DMS in the sheet while the Payrillium report marks their operation mode as N/A; K1-286 is critical, so that single choice is what keeps the whole operation-mode matrix red.
