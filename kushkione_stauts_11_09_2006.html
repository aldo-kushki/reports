Summary

Where we stand. 31 clients in production, of which 28 are Standalone and only 3 run on an integrated mode — and those three are all in Chile. In production by country: Colombia 11, Chile 9, Mexico 6, Peru 5, plus one churned client in Peru. Another 10 clients are integrating, and there the balance flips: 8 of them are integrated and 2 Standalone, so the integrated side of the product is still ahead of us rather than behind us.

Blockers. Three critical issues are open — critical meaning immediate or urgent — and all three are already fixed and shipping on 15/09/2026: one sits on DMS, which every operation mode depends on, and the other two reach every service, which is what keeps the readiness matrices red. Ten more high issues are reported for information without affecting approval: three ship on 15/09/2026, three on 25/09/2026 and four have no release date yet.

Releases. 37 tickets across the last four releases, 30 approved on first review (81%), 6 calendar days on average to production. Quality and speed are improving: 74% approved and 17 days to production on 21/08, versus 90% and 4 days on 03/09; hotfixes shipped in 1–2 days.

Pipeline. Nine projected clients: Chile 5, Peru 4, Mexico 3, Colombia 1 — mostly Standalone and App to App Sync. Cloud Async has no pipeline anywhere, and Peru has no projected client on App to App Sync beyond Standalone-first partners.

Reporting criteria

Purpose of this block. It records every rule agreed for this report so it can be rebuilt from scratch from the spreadsheet without any other context. Sections, in order: Summary (a short narrative only — the KPI cards were removed on 2026-09-11, so no metric blocks belong here) → Country status → Product readiness by operation mode → Product readiness by service → Release status → Critical issues detected → this block.

Source. Google spreadsheet 1qHi_HphqZV8yhYy0Gza1FZMRX9xx9N9qTex1pCbSb0o, tabs Clientes (gid 0), Pipeline, Issues (gid 1543440227) and releases (gid 130905531). Read them as CSV through /gviz/tq?tqx=out:csv&gid=… (the /export?format=csv endpoint fails). Sheet dates are MM/DD/YYYY; this report always shows DD/MM/YYYY. The report is a single self-contained HTML file, in English, and always says “clients”, never “merchants”; all figures are rendered from the REPORT_DATA object, so refreshing data means editing that object only.

Countries and columns. Countries are Mexico, Chile, Colombia and Peru (the sheet writes México and Perú). Operation modes are Standalone, Cloud Sync, Cloud Async, App to App Sync and App to App Async. The readiness matrices show only the first four: App to App Async is excluded because both apps run on the same terminal, so asynchronous calls add little value there and gaps in that mode are expected rather than a problem to solve. Services keep the four report names Charge, Preauth & Capture, Refunds and Other Services, mapped from the sheet as Charge → Charge, Void → Refunds, Other Integration Services → Other Services.

Client counting. Statuses are Producción → Production, Integración → Integration, Abandona → Churned, plus Pipeline from its own tab. Within a country each client name counts once; regional totals count unique client names, so a client present in several countries counts once regionally and the sum across countries is higher. A client with several operation modes counts once in each declared mode, so the sum across modes can exceed the client count. Churned clients are excluded from every count and only mentioned in the note of the first KPI. Pipeline clients never count towards readiness or coverage, because they have not started integrating.

Readiness criteria (per country × operation mode and per country × service). C1: at least one client in production with that mode or service. C2: those clients ran at least 200 transactions in the last 7 days. C3: no critical issue open for that country and that mode or service. States: green ✓ when all three are met, amber ! when at least one is missing, red ✕ when no criterion is met or when critical issues exist (issues force red on their own), and violet ⊘ “not started” when there are no clients at all in integration or production. That last state is a gap, not a neutral value: nothing has been piloted in that combination yet, which is why it does not use a plain grey dash. Red and amber cells print every reason under the icon, one short line each, and for issues that line is only the count plus the date they are all resolved (or “resolution date TBD”). The ticket-level detail belongs to the panel that opens when the cell is clicked: clients in production and in integration, clients in pipeline, weekly transactions against the threshold, a ✓/✕ checklist of the three criteria, and the list of open issues with ID, name, criticality, status, release date and the note “(DMS, applies to all operation modes)” where it applies. Each matrix has 16 cells: 4 countries × 4 operation modes and 4 countries × 4 services. There is no aggregate coverage percentage in the report: the Summary “pilot coverage” KPIs, which counted cells meeting criteria 1 and 2 while ignoring issues, were removed with the rest of the metric cards on 2026-09-11.

Transaction assumption in force. The columns “MIDs Asociados”, “Transacciones en los últimos 7 días”, “TPV últimos 7 días” and “% de aceptación” exist in the Clientes tab but are empty. Wherever a figure depends on them the report shows an ⏳ “Awaiting data” marker instead of inventing a number, and criterion C2 is assumed met so it never blocks a cell on missing data. When the columns are loaded, remove the assumption and evaluate C2 with the real figures; the aggregation rule is to sum transactions of the clients behind each cell, and a client transacting in several services cannot be split across them, so a per-service figure is needed before the service matrix reports real volume.

Issues. Critical means Immediate or Urgent (constant CRITICAL): these are the only issues that fail criterion 3, and the only ones counted in the “open critical issues” KPI, whose note breaks them down by status and by release date. High issues are reported in the issues section for information and never affect approval; together with the critical ones they form the reported set (constant REPORTED). Medium tickets stay in the tracker and out of this report. This split was agreed on 2026-09-11 so the matrices reflect real blockers instead of the whole backlog — do not merge the two lists again. Status is irrelevant to whether an issue blocks: Fixed still has to pass QA and ship, so only the release date matters. An empty release date means TBD (to be defined) and ranks after every real date, so a cell that holds a TBD ticket cannot show a resolution date and reads “Resolution date TBD” instead of “All resolved by DD/MM/YYYY”. An issue applies to a cell when its country list contains the country and its mode or service list contains the column. DMS rule: DMS is stored literally in the sheet but is not a column here, because every operation mode requires it, so an issue whose mode is DMS is expanded to all operation modes (constant DMS_BLOCKS_CELLS in the code, set to true; setting it to false would instead treat DMS issues as a cross-cutting note). Issues with no mode or service cannot be placed in any cell and appear in the issues table only.

Country status. One table, two rows per country plus a regional pair: Standalone covers clients using the Standalone mode and Integrated covers clients using Cloud Sync, Cloud Async, App to App Sync or App to App Async; a client using both is counted in both rows, so the two rows can add up to more than the country total. Clients in production, in integration and pipeline split by segment; transactions, TPV and approval rate are ⏳ awaiting data, and show “–” where the segment has no clients at all rather than a marker, because there is nothing to measure. “Operation modes live” and “Services live” require at least one client in production, not just integrating, and the regional row shows the union of everything live in any country. When the approval rate arrives, the regional figure is weighted by transactions.

Release status. Days to production = production shipping date minus first delivery date, in calendar days. First-pass approval = tickets approved on the first review ÷ total tickets. Average time to production averages those days over the releases listed, with the release-candidate versus hotfix split shown in the KPI note. Shipping to production does not imply a per-terminal deployment status.

Open data gaps and assumptions. Transaction, TPV, MID and approval columns are empty, as described above. The Clientes tab lists Justo once per country since 2026-09-11, but the Mexico and Colombia rows have no operation mode or service, so this report assumes both mirror Justo Chile (App to App Sync; Charge, Refunds, Other Services) — replace the assumption once the sheet is filled. K1-304 lists all four countries while its title mentions Mexico only; it is reported as all four. The five tickets with no date carry an empty cell in the sheet instead of the literal “TBD”, and are read as TBD here.
